A practical starting point for choosing solar equipment

Solar equipment works as a system. Before comparing panel wattage or battery capacity, list the appliances you need to run and the hours you expect to use them. This guide helps you prepare useful questions for a qualified installer; it is not a substitute for a site assessment or electrical design.

1. Estimate your daily energy use

For each appliance, multiply its rated power in watts by the number of hours it runs in a day. Add the results to estimate energy use in watt-hours (Wh). Use the appliance nameplate or manufacturer documentation where possible: labels and real operating loads can differ.

Daily energy (Wh) = appliance power (W) × hours used per day

For example, a 60W fan running for 5 hours uses about 300Wh before system losses. Repeat the calculation for every appliance you want powered. For motors and compressors, also note starting surge requirements; their initial demand can be higher than their normal running wattage.

2. Check the full system, not just the panel

Solar panels

Panel output depends on sunlight, orientation, temperature, shading, and installation. The nameplate wattage is a laboratory rating, not a promise of daily energy. Ask how the proposed array is expected to perform at your site.

Battery storage

Compare the battery's voltage and capacity with the inverter and the energy you need after sunset. Usable energy depends on chemistry, depth-of-discharge limits, age, temperature, and system losses. Do not compare amp-hour figures across different voltages without converting to watt-hours.

Inverter and charge controller

Check continuous power, surge rating, DC voltage, controller current limits, and supported battery chemistry against the rest of the system. Products with similar headline ratings are not automatically compatible.

3. Plan the installation and total cost

Ask a qualified installer to consider roof or ground-mount structure, cable length and size, protection devices, earthing, ventilation, shading, and applicable electrical rules. Request an itemised quote covering equipment, mounting, wiring, installation, delivery, and any maintenance terms before committing.

Questions to ask before ordering

  • Are the panel, controller, battery, and inverter voltages compatible?
  • What appliances can the system support at the same time, including startup surges?
  • What usable battery energy and expected runtime are included in the estimate?
  • What exactly is included in the product price, warranty, delivery, and installation quote?
  • Are the products in stock, and what is the confirmed delivery timeline for my location?

Electrical and rooftop solar work can present shock, fire, and fall hazards. Have system sizing and installation checked by appropriately qualified professionals. Product availability, manufacturer specifications, delivery charges, and prices can change; confirm them with the seller before payment.
